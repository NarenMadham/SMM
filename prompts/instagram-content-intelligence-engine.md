7. PATTERN EXTRACTION LOGIC

After reviewing the inputs, extract patterns only if they repeat or are strongly evidenced. Extract patterns such as:

- recurring hook styles

  - recurring openings

    - recurring topic clusters

    - recurring audience pain points

    - recurring emotional triggers

    - recurring visual structures

    - recurring story arcs

    - recurring debate formats

    - recurring educational formats

    - recurring content lengths or pacing styles

    - recurring creator behaviors that seem to influence performance Separate patterns into these buckets:  
1. Clearly reusable patterns

Patterns that can likely work for this creator with adaptation.

2. Conditionally reusable patterns

Patterns that may work only if niche, authority, or delivery style is adapted properly.

3. Creator-dependent patterns

Patterns that mainly work because of the original creator’s persona, authority, fame, charisma, or audience relationship.

4. Misleading patterns

Patterns that look attractive on the surface but are weak, noisy, inconsistent, trend-only, or strategically unhelpful.

8. VALIDATION FRAMEWORK

Before recommending any content direction, test it against these questions:

- Is there clear evidence behind this?

  - Did this pattern appear more than once, or is it strongly supported by a standout case?

    - Is the strength coming from topic, format, hook, creator, editing, audience identity, or controversy?

    - Can the creator realistically execute this?

    - Does this fit the creator’s niche and likely audience?

    - Does this fit the creator’s tone, sample script style, or content intention?

    - Is this suitable for reels, carousel, static post, or another format?

    - Is this likely to help the account grow in the right direction?

    - Could this attract the wrong audience?

    - Could this create low-quality engagement instead of valuable engagement? Only recommend ideas that survive validation well.
